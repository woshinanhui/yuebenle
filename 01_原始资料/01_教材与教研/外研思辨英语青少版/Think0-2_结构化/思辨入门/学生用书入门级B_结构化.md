- **RULE:** We use *4* to talk about ability. The negative form is *5*. We don't use do/does with can in questions or negative forms.

**Positive / Negative table:**
- Positive: I / You / We / He / She / It / They **can** jump.
- Negative: I / You / We / He / She / It / They **can** (cannot) jump.
- **Questions:** Yes, I/you/we/he/she/it/they **can.** — No, I/you/we/he/she/it/they **can't.**
- 空格：you/we/he/she/it/they (can't) ... jump?

#### 正文：AN AMAZING LAST FOUR MINUTES

It's 15th February at Greece Athena High School, New York. Jason McElwain is an 18-year-old student. Jason is autistic. He loves basketball and he helps with the school basketball team. Jason practises hard every day, but he never plays in the team.

Tonight is the last game of the season. It's Jason's last chance to play a game with the team, but for 44 minutes, he sits on the bench and watches.

The team wins, and Jason is a real hero. It's an amazing night for him!

Soon Jason starts to become famous. People in his town know who he is. Jason writes a book and some people want to make a film about him. He becomes assistant coach to the high school basketball team.

Now Jason works hard to help other autistic people. He travels across the US to help get money for autism research.

**1.** Look at the examples of the present continuous. Find other examples in the microblogs on page 13. Then choose the correct words to complete the rule and the table.

- **RULE:** We use the present continuous to talk about things that happen every day / are happening now. We form the present continuous with the present tense of be / have and the -ing form of the main verb.

**Positive / Negative / Question table:**
- Positive: I'm (am) listening. | You/We/They are (are) listening. | He/She/It is (is) listening.
- Negative: I'm not (am not) listening. | You/We/They aren't (are not) listening. | He/She/It isn't (is not) listening.
- Question + short answer: Am I listening? Yes, I am. / No, I'm not. | Are you/we/they listening? Yes, you/we/they are. / No, you/we/they aren't. | Is he/she/it listening? Yes, he/she/it is. / No, he/she/it isn't.

**Dances around the world**

**THE TANGO**
The Tango is the national dance of Argentina, and every year on 11th December people in Buenos Aires celebrate the Day of the Tango. They do the tango in dance halls, parks and squares all over the city. The dance is usually performed by a man and a woman. The man often wears smart trousers and a shirt. The woman often wears a beautiful dress. The dance is often very interesting to watch.

**IRISH DANCING**
There are lots of Irish dances, but perhaps the most famous one is the 'step dance'. One or more dancers perform the step dance. The dancers only move their legs. They move them very quickly. The top half of the body doesn't move. Dancers usually wear traditional Irish costumes. The music they dance to is played by an Irish folk band. They play instruments like the fiddle and the whistle.

**THE WHIRLING DERVISH**
The Whirling Dervish is a dance from Turkey. There is a group of twelve men who dance. They wear traditional costumes with big skirts. They often all wear white, but sometimes the skirts are different colours. There is another group of twelve musicians. They play traditional Turkish instruments. As the music plays, the dancers spin around in circles, getting faster and faster. The dance is very popular with tourists.

## 附录 · GET IT RIGHT!（第 60–61 页）

### UNIT 1 – can/can't

Learners sometimes use the wrong form of the verb when they use can and to.
> We use the infinitive without to after can.
> He can play the piano. × He can to play the piano.
> We use to + infinitive after want and need.
> They want to know the answer. × They want know the answer.

**Correct the mistakes in the sentences.**
- 0 He needs do that. → He needs to do that.
- 1 Can you to speak Spanish?
- 2 We want do some shopping.
- 3 You need clean your room.
- 4 I need eat something. I'm hungry!
- 5 I can't to do my homework.
- 6 Alex wants do everything.

### UNIT 2 – like / don't like + verb + -ing

Learners sometimes use the wrong form of the verb where -ing is required.
> We use the -ing form of verbs after the verbs like, don't like, love and hate.
> √ I like playing tennis.
> × I Hike play tennis.      [原文 OCR 混排，修正为：× I like play tennis.]
> × I like to playing tennis.

**Correct the mistakes in the sentences.**
- 0 I like read books. → I like reading books.
- 1 I like sing and dancing.
- 2 We love go shopping.
- 3 She likes to wearing green clothes.
- 4 They don't like play basketball.
- 5 Paul doesn't hate study.
- 6 Anna likes to wearing white clothes.

### UNIT 3 – must/mustn't/can/would

Learners often have trouble spelling modal verbs can't, must, would and mustn't.

**Correct the spelling mistakes in the sentences.**
- 0 Wold you like to go with me? → Would you like to go with me?
- 1 I cant find the coffee.
- 2 You mast learn English.
- 3 I woud like to do some shopping.
- 4 You musn't use this website.
- 5 Woudn't you like some more?
- 6 We mussn't be late.

### UNIT 4 – was / wasn't, were / weren't

Learners sometimes confuse was and were. Was, wasn't, were and weren't all have to agree with the subject.
> The jeans were very beautiful. × The jeans was very beautiful.

**Tick (√) the correct sentences and cross (×) the incorrect ones. Correct the mistakes.**
- 0 There was a lot of people. × → There were a lot of people.
- 1 We was at Dan's house all night.
- 2 There was a lot of food.
- 3 Wasn't you there?
- 4 I were happy to see you at the weekend.
- 5 How many people were at your house?
- 6 Last night there were a party on the beach.
- 7 He was my friend at school.
- 8 Katie and Jo was there.

### UNIT 5 – Past simple (irregular verbs)

Learners sometimes use the wrong forms of irregular verbs in the past simple or misspell them.
> I paid a lot of money. × I payed a lot of money.

**Correct the mistakes in the sentences.**
- 0 I haved a holiday. → I had a holiday.
- 1 I maked a lot of friends.
- 2 She gived me a lot of presents.
- 3 Jack and Al taked photographs.
- 4 There some problems with his work. [注：原文混合练习]
- 5 I came home late yesterday.
- 6 He swimmed very fast.
- 7 They goed to the cinema.
- 8 Helen mad some food.

### UNIT 5 – Past simple (negative)

Learners sometimes use the present simple negative where the past simple is required.
> I didn't find the answer before the end of the exam. × I don't find the answer before the end of the exam.

**Choose the correct options.**
- 0 We don't / didn't go to the game last week.
- 1 I didn't / don't need any help at the moment.
- 2 I bought some T-shirts but I didn't / don't buy any tops.
- 3 I went to a mobile phone shop but I didn't / don't like the phones there.
- 4 A: Do you like chicken? B: No, I didn't / don't eat meat.
- 5 I had a lot of presents but he didn't / don't give me one.
- 6 We didn't / don't usually go on holiday because we like being at home.

### UNIT 6 – Comparative adjectives

Learners often use more and -er in the same sentence when only one of these forms is required.
> We form comparative adjectives by adding -er if the adjective has one syllable (or two syllables ending in -y), and by using more if the adjective has more than two syllables. We don't use more and -er together.
> This one is bigger than that one. × This one is more bigger than that one. / × This one is more big than that one.

**Correct the mistakes in the sentences.**
- 0 The train is more cheap than the plane. → The train is cheaper than the plane.
- 1 He is more healthier than he was last year.
- 2 Henry is more older than his brother.
- 3 My mobile phone is more newer than my brother's.
- 4 Basketball is more good than baseball.
- 5 I was more happy than Joe at the end of the game.
- 6 Henry's brother is more friendlier than Henry.
- 7 I think Maths is more easy than English.

**than with comparative adjectives**

Learners sometimes use then or that where than is required with comparative adjectives.
> Colin is older than Oliver. × Colin is older then Oliver. / × Colin is older that Oliver.

**Complete the sentences with then, that or than.**
- 0 He can run faster ___ me.
- 1 It is much better ___ your mobile phone.
- 2 Call me ___ I thought.
- 3 It is more ___ is my book.
- 4 ___
- 5 This one is better ___ one.
- 6 We danced and watched a film.
